# Executive decision memo

Help an executive make a clear, timely decision and secure the necessary agreement. Produce a one-page memo that puts the decision first, compares credible options, explains the recommendation, makes risks visible, and clarifies who must agree by when. The executive owns the decision and approval process; do not imply that anyone has agreed or that a decision has been made unless the user says so.

## Procedure

1. **Clarify the decision.** Ask what exact decision is needed, why now, the desired outcome, and the latest useful decision date. Establish the decision owner and the people or roles whose agreement is required. Distinguish approvers from people to consult or inform.
2. **Gather the basis.** Request the viable options (including maintaining the status quo if relevant), decision criteria, evidence, constraints, costs or trade-offs, dependencies, and consequences of delay. Ask what is already known versus assumed. Ask for a source, amount, or date when a material claim is unclear.
3. **Test the options.** Compare options consistently against the stated criteria. Identify the strongest reason for and against each viable option. Check whether the recommendation follows from the evidence and criteria; if it does not, point out the gap rather than rationalizing it. Do not invent facts, probabilities, costs, commitments, or stakeholder positions.
4. **Make agreement actionable.** For each required approver, state the role or name, what they need to agree to, and the date by which they must do so. Include an owner for the next step when supplied. If roles, dates, or approvals are unknown, mark them as explicit placeholders and flag them after the memo; never present assumed support as agreement.
5. **Draft and tighten.** Write for a busy executive: direct, neutral, specific, and easy to scan. Keep the memo to one page (about 400–500 words maximum); prioritize decision-relevant information. Lead with the decision and recommendation, use a compact options comparison, and state risks with mitigations or triggers only when known. Put unresolved material questions after the draft, and omit that section when there are none.

## Output format

# Decision memo: [decision in a few words]

**To:** [decision-maker / required approvers]  
**From:** [owner]  
**Decision needed by:** [date]

**Decision** — One precise question or choice to resolve, with brief context on why it is needed now.

**Recommendation** — State the preferred option and the most important evidence-based reasons. Name the outcome or trade-off it prioritizes.

**Options considered** — A compact table with columns `Option | Advantages | Trade-offs`. Include the recommendation and other credible options; state the status quo if it is a real alternative. Be fair to each option.

**Risks and mitigations** — The most material risks of the recommended option, likely impact or trigger, and mitigation or owner when known. Say when material risks or mitigations are not yet assessed.

**Agreement and next steps** — List each required approver (name or role), what they must agree to, and by when. Add the decision/implementation owner and next step if known. Keep consults and notifications distinct from required approvals.

**Open questions** — Only material missing facts, unconfirmed approvers, or deadlines that prevent a reliable decision; omit if none.

## Example of good output

# Decision memo: Renew the analytics contract

**To:** COO; Finance lead  
**From:** VP, Operations  
**Decision needed by:** 15 June 2026

**Decision** — Should we renew the analytics platform for one year before the current contract expires on 30 June?

**Recommendation** — Renew for one year. The team relies on the platform for weekly operating reviews, and a short renewal avoids a service gap while the replacement evaluation is completed. This favors continuity over an immediate switch; confirm the final renewal price before approval.

**Options considered**

| Option | Advantages | Trade-offs |
|---|---|---|
| Renew for one year (recommended) | Maintains current reporting | Extends spend; renewal price needs confirmation |
| Do not renew; move to spreadsheets | Avoids renewal commitment | Manual reporting workload and continuity risk have not been quantified |

**Risks and mitigations** — Renewal could lock in an unattractive price. Confirm the quote before signing; alternatives and switching costs have not yet been assessed.

**Agreement and next steps**
- COO: approve the one-year renewal by 15 June.
- Finance lead: confirm budget and renewal price by 12 June.
- VP, Operations: obtain the quote and return with the confirmed terms.

**Open questions** — What is the renewal price and is it within the approved budget?

This example shows the format, not facts to reuse. Use only information supplied for the actual decision.

## Worked example (invented): Select a research vendor

# Decision memo: Select a customer research vendor

**To:** COO  
**From:** Head of Product  
**Decision needed by:** 10 July 2026

**Decision** — Which vendor should run customer interviews before the Q3 roadmap is finalized?

**Recommendation** — Choose Northstar Research: its proposed four-week schedule meets the roadmap deadline, and its fixed $30,000 fee is within budget.

**Options considered**

| Option | Advantages | Trade-offs |
|---|---|---|
| Northstar Research (recommended) | Four-week schedule meets the roadmap deadline | $30,000 fee |
| Signal Insights | Lower $24,000 fee | Six-week schedule means results arrive after roadmap decisions |

**Risks and mitigations** — Northstar's schedule leaves little room for recruiting delays; Product will confirm the participant list before kickoff.

**Agreement and next steps**
- COO: approve the $30,000 engagement by 10 July.
- Head of Product: finalize the participant list by 13 July and start onboarding.

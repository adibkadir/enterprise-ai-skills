# Quarterly board update

Turn an executive's source material into a candid, decision-oriented quarterly update that directors can scan before a meeting. The executive owns the facts, judgment, and final communication; do not imply that a draft has been approved or sent.

## Procedure

1. **Gather inputs.** Ask for the quarter and company context, the approved plan or targets, actual results, prior-quarter or year-over-year comparisons if useful, notable wins, misses and their causes, current risks, and the board's requested decisions or help. Ask for units, dates, and metric definitions where they are unclear. Invite relevant context such as prior commitments, runway, and outlook, but do not require sensitive details that are not needed.
2. **Check the evidence.** Separate reported facts from interpretation and proposals. For each headline metric, show actual, plan, and variance (and units); state the comparison period where relevant. Calculate variance only when the inputs and basis are clear. Flag conflicting, missing, or ambiguous data as questions or explicit placeholders. Never invent figures, reasons, forecasts, owners, or board requests.
3. **Find the signal.** Select the few results that best explain the quarter and the outlook. Connect misses to their supported causes and consequences. Distinguish root causes from symptoms and identify what management is doing next only when supplied. Rank risks by likely impact and urgency, and state mitigations or decisions needed when known.
4. **Draft for directors.** Lead with the overall conclusion, then give a compact scorecard and the sections in the output format below. Use plain language, direct ownership, and specific dates or amounts. Be candid and balanced, not promotional. Keep the main update to about one page (roughly 500–700 words); prioritize over completeness and use bullets for scanability.
5. **Surface gaps.** After the draft, list only the high-impact questions or placeholders that prevent a reliable board-ready version. Make each request concrete. If all necessary information is present, do not add needless questions.

## Output format

# [Company] — [Quarter, year] board update

**Headline:** One or two sentences on performance against plan and the most important implication for the next quarter.

**Headline numbers** — a table with columns `Metric | Actual | Plan | Variance | Context`. Include only decision-relevant metrics; mark unavailable values clearly rather than guessing.

**What went well** — two to four bullets. State the result and why it matters; include evidence or a link/reference if provided.

**What did not go to plan — and why** — two to four bullets. State the miss, supported cause, impact, and corrective action or owner/timing if provided.

**Top risks** — up to three ranked bullets, each with the risk, potential impact/timing, mitigation, and any trigger or help needed when known.

**Decisions and help needed from the board** — numbered asks. For each, state the specific decision/introduction/help, the recommended option and rationale if supplied, and the deadline or consequence of delay. If there are no asks, say so explicitly.

**Open questions** — include only material gaps that require executive input before circulation; omit if none.

## Example of good output

# Northstar — Q2 2026 board update

**Headline:** Revenue reached $12.0M, 4% below plan, while gross margin exceeded plan by 2 points. The revenue gap is concentrated in delayed enterprise launches; recovering those launches is the key Q3 priority.

**Headline numbers**

| Metric | Actual | Plan | Variance | Context |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---|
| Revenue | $12.0M | $12.5M | -$0.5M (-4%) | Two enterprise launches moved to Q3 |
| Gross margin | 72% | 70% | +2 pts | Above plan |

**What went well**
- Gross margin reached 72%, two points above plan.

**What did not go to plan — and why**
- Revenue was 4% below plan after two enterprise launches moved to Q3; confirm the revised launch dates before circulation.

**Top risks**
1. Further launch delays could push revenue into a later quarter; management is tracking launch readiness weekly.

**Decisions and help needed from the board**
1. No decisions or help requested this quarter.

This example illustrates format only; use only facts supplied for the executive's actual update.

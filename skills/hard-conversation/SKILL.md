# Hard conversation

Help the executive prepare a private, one-page conversation sheet. Draft for the executive to review and deliver; do not make employment decisions or send messages. Ask only for details needed to prepare the conversation.

## Procedure

1. **Identify the purpose.** Ask whether this meeting is to give performance feedback or communicate a role elimination. If unclear, clarify before drafting. Keep coaching separate from communicating an elimination decision.
2. **Performance feedback.** Ask for the agreed expectation, a specific observation, its impact, and the change sought. State what happened without guessing at motives. Draft opening words that connect the expectation, observation, and known impact. Ask for the person's perspective and pause before discussing the change. Close with support, a specific next action and owner, and a check-in to review progress. If a date or commitment is missing, propose agreeing it in the meeting rather than presenting it as settled.
3. **Role elimination.** Ask whether the decision is final and what the executive is approved to share. If either is unclear, do not draft an announcement. Instead, give opening words for a private HR conversation, such as “Before I speak with the employee, can you confirm whether the decision is final and exactly what I can share?” Have the executive resolve those questions with HR before communicating the decision. If both are confirmed and the elimination is approved for disclosure, lead directly with “Your role is being eliminated.” Pause, acknowledge the response, and invite questions. Provide only confirmed next steps, including who will do what and when if known. Do not suggest that better performance could reverse the decision or speculate about timing, severance, or benefits.
4. **Check the wording.** Mark missing facts as `[confirm …]` in the prep sheet; never fill gaps with assumptions. If a material fact is missing, label affected wording as not ready to deliver. Check unresolved legal or policy questions with HR or counsel; these examples are not legal safeguards.

### Avoid / say instead

- **Performance:** Avoid “You don’t care about deadlines.” Say “We agreed on [date]; I received it on [date]. What got in the way?”
- **Performance:** Avoid “Just do better.” Say “For the next report, let’s agree on a delivery date and when you’ll flag a risk of missing it.”
- **Role elimination:** Avoid “I’m sure you’ll get severance.” Say “I don’t have confirmed terms to share. Let’s take that question to HR.”
- **Role elimination:** Avoid “Maybe we can revisit this if your numbers improve.” Once a final decision is approved for disclosure, say “The decision to eliminate your role is final. I’ll pause here for your questions.”

## Output format

# Private prep — [meeting purpose]

**Objective:** What the meeting should accomplish. If the elimination gate is not met, make this an HR preparation sheet, not an employee script.

**Opening words:** Two or three plainspoken sentences for the intended audience.

**Questions and responses:** A question, an explicit pause for the other person to respond, and a brief way to acknowledge their response without guessing what they will say or promising an outcome.

**Follow-up:** State the next action, owner, and timing where known. For feedback, include support and a check-in; for elimination, include only confirmed logistics. If logistics are missing, tell the executive privately what to confirm with HR, not what to promise the employee.

**Facts to confirm:** List material gaps or say “None.”

## Example of good output

Fictional performance example; do not reuse its facts.

# Private prep — Weekly report deadlines

**Objective:** Get the weekly report in before the Monday planning meeting.

**Opening words:** “We agreed the report would arrive Friday by noon. It arrived Monday afternoon, so we planned without the latest figures. What got in the way?”

**Questions and responses:** Pause and listen. Then ask, “What would help you get the next report in by Friday noon?”

**Follow-up:** Propose that you review the data dependencies together on Thursday. Ask the report owner to flag any blocker at that review and submit the report by Friday noon. Agree to check at Monday’s planning meeting whether the report arrived in time.

**Facts to confirm:** Whether Thursday works for the review.
